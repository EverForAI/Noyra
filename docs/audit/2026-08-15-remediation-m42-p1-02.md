# M42 P1-02 Production Integrity Registry Remediation

Date: 2026-08-17

Baseline: `main` / `87e059a` plus the verified M41/M42 remediation work.

## Scope

The read-only audit found that the integrity registry existed as library code but was not a reliable
production gate. Startup and periodic execution were incomplete, the watchdog could be blocked by a
checker, normal and corrupt states were not consistently separated, and many durable domains were not
registered or did not actually read their owned tables. A P0 in one of those domains could therefore
remain invisible or fail to drive the durable quarantine and safe-pause policy.

P1-02 has two independent contracts:

- **A - production execution:** versioned checks run before startup recovery and on a bounded periodic
  schedule, with a real hard deadline and shutdown behavior that cannot leave a checker or writer alive.
- **B - complete durable coverage and policy:** every in-scope durable domain is read and validated;
  P1 availability/resource outcomes remain distinguishable from proven P0 corruption; proven P0 state
  persists through quarantine and follows the configured alert or safe-pause policy.

This remediation does not close P1-12. Project execution results, artifact bytes, acceptance evidence,
and output-specific validators still need their own digest and semantic-validation contract. It also
does not implement P1-11 keyring rotation or cloud read-through restore.

## Production execution boundary

`src/noyra/core/integrity.py` now owns the built-in, versioned registry and the runtime controller.
The registry has 37 checks across core, mind, sleep, interaction, capability, world, learning,
cognition, model, and knowledge domains.

Production execution now:

- acquires subject ownership without performing mutable restart recovery;
- runs the startup gate before recovery, policy synchronization, secret repair, exporter recovery, or
  cognition starts;
- runs periodic deep checks on the watchdog schedule and exposes the same registry through the manual
  path;
- creates an independent `spawn` child process for a built-in audit;
- opens the existing SQLite database through an OS-enforced `mode=ro` URI and `query_only` snapshot;
- holds one SQLite read snapshot across the selected checks;
- applies per-check row, value, byte, file, detail, and SQLite progress bounds;
- uses a wall-clock deadline that includes child startup and checker execution;
- preserves completed P0/P1 results when a later check times out;
- cooperates with service shutdown and otherwise performs bounded join, terminate, and kill cleanup;
  and
- leaves no live `noyra-integrity-audit` process when the call returns.

Startup cannot silently initialize a missing durable subject when the service is configured to open an
existing subject. Corrupt lifecycle state, malformed watchdog state, stale or upgraded registry state,
and a subject-ID mismatch fail closed. Quarantine survives restart until a complete current-registry run
passes.

## Finding and safe-pause policy

Registry reports retain check version, domain, tier, status, severity, reason, duration, examined rows
and bytes, bounded details, snapshot marker, selection, and deferred coverage.

- `off` disables scheduled enforcement without rewriting durable state.
- `alert` persists and reports findings without changing lifecycle state.
- `pause` records proven P0 quarantine and enters the model-free safe-pause path.
- resource, key, provider, and verification-envelope failures remain P1 degraded or incomplete rather
  than being rewritten as SQLite/P0 corruption.
- service shutdown is a cooperative control path, not an integrity finding.

The safe-pause fallback is itself append-only audited. Restart, registry upgrade, partial reports, and
concurrent periodic execution cannot clear quarantine or resume cognition without the required complete
healthy run.

## Durable-domain completion

A final SQLite authorizer audit ran the healthy schema-33 manual registry. All 37 checks were `ok`, 109
tables were read, and every one of the 11 previously missed durable tables was reached by its owner:

| Registry check | Newly proven table reads |
|---|---|
| `core.actions` | `audit_records`, `training_policies`, `training_records`, `training_exports` |
| `cognition.autonomous_projects` | `autonomous_project_resource_uses`, `autonomous_project_assistance_requests`, `autonomous_project_sleep_reflections` |
| `model.resources` | `cognitive_resource_group_revisions`, `cognitive_resource_key_events` |
| `sleep.state` | `fatigue_transitions` |
| `world.state` | `observation_content_segments` |

The authorizer coverage is now a regression test, so a future registry edit cannot drop those reads while
leaving only a check ID in the inventory.

The completed validators enforce strict persisted scalar types, finite numbers, canonical JSON, hashes,
subject ownership, append-only history, revision ordering, lifecycle transitions, and current-state
reconciliation. Specific additions include:

- model resource group revisions and key events, including reverse cross-subject ownership;
- fatigue history even when no current fatigue row exists;
- observation archive segment metadata, bounded file reads/decompression, hashes, bidirectional
  segment/observation references, and total archive-byte accounting;
- core audit and training policy/record/export provenance plus audit-history reconciliation;
- autonomous-project resource sources, assistance requests, sleep reflections, artifact-source links,
  hashes, JSON, numeric bounds, and reverse ownership; and
- epistemic-review and search-provider-use queries that include referenced observations, model calls,
  configurations, and actions, preventing a foreign row from hiding by using another subject ID.

The review also confirmed that the remaining non-read classifications are deliberate: revision/history
children covered through their verified parent and SQLite foreign keys, rebuildable FTS shadow tables,
operational/runtime tables, and deferred `storage_archives` coverage. They were not relabeled as missing
durable domains.
